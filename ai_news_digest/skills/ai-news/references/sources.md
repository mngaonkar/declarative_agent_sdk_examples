# Trusted AI News Sources

Use this as a guide for where to look and what to trust when building the digest. Favor primary sources first, then reputable outlets, then everything else with appropriate skepticism.

## Primary sources (highest trust — go here first)

These are the people actually doing the thing. A lab's own post beats any rewrite of it.

- **Lab / company blogs**: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Google AI, Meta AI, Mistral, Microsoft Research, Nvidia, Cohere, Stability AI, xAI, Hugging Face.
- **Research**: arXiv (cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.LG), conference proceedings (NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL), Papers with Code.
- **Official filings & docs**: SEC filings for public-company news, government/regulator sites for policy (EU Commission, US executive orders, FTC, UK AISI), official model cards and release notes.

## Reputable secondary outlets

Good for confirmation, context, and stories that haven't been posted by a primary source.

- **General tech with strong AI desks**: The Verge, Ars Technica, TechCrunch, Wired, MIT Technology Review, IEEE Spectrum.
- **Business/finance**: Reuters, Bloomberg, The Information, Financial Times, CNBC.
- **AI-focused**: Import AI (Jack Clark), The Batch (DeepLearning.AI), Ben's Bites, TLDR AI, Stratechery (analysis).

## Use with skepticism

Can surface real signal early, but verify before reporting as fact.

- Social platforms (X/Twitter, LinkedIn, Reddit r/MachineLearning, r/LocalLLaMA): great for spotting that something happened; confirm specifics against a primary or reputable source before summarizing.
- Aggregators and AI-generated news sites: often rewrites of an original — trace back to and cite the original instead.
- Single-source rumors and "leaks": label clearly as unconfirmed, or hold them out.

## Quick heuristics

- If a story only exists on one low-quality site, treat it as unconfirmed.
- When a headline and the article body disagree, trust the body.
- Prefer the source closest to the event: the announcement over the news story over the tweet about the news story.
- For benchmark or performance claims, link the source of the number, not a secondhand characterization of it.
